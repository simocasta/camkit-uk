Why CamKIT

Acute soft-tissue knee injuries — ruptures of the anterior cruciate and other ligaments, meniscal tears — are easy to miss at first presentation. Pain and effusion limit examination, radiographs exclude fracture but say nothing about soft tissue, and MRI and specialist review are scarce resources. The result is a well-documented pattern of delayed diagnosis, repeat attendance and late referral, with the risk of further damage to an unstable knee in the meantime.

CamKIT was developed at the University of Cambridge to close that gap using only information already available in an urgent-care consultation. A Delphi study of orthopaedic and emergency clinicians established consensus on the diagnostic challenge and the highest-value features in history and examination; those features were refined into twelve yes/no findings that sum to a simple additive score, validated against clinically significant soft-tissue injury; and an 18-month feasibility study at an urgent treatment centre examined how the tool fits real-world triage.

Each finding scores one point. The total places the patient in one of three traffic-light bands, each mapped to a next step: Low (0–3) — discharge with safety-netting advice; Medium (4–6) — conservative management with planned reassessment; High (7–12) — refer for MRI or specialist review. The banding is deliberately cautious at the rule-out end: in the validation data, no patient with a clinically significant injury scored in the Low band.

Intended use

CamKIT may be used within a clinical service as part of the assessment process. However, CamKIT is a research tool and has not been approved by any regulatory authority as a clinical diagnostic device.

As such, the CamKIT score should not be used in isolation to make, support, or determine a clinical diagnosis or other clinical decision. Any clinical decisions should be based on the clinician's professional judgement and a comprehensive assessment, with CamKIT serving only as one source of information alongside other evidence.

It is intended for use by healthcare professionals, in the context of patients aged 16 and over presenting with acute knee injury after fracture has been excluded (e.g. Ottawa Knee Rule) per local pathways.

Developed and validated on clinician-completed data — not validated for patient self-assessment.
